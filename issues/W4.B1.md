# [W4.B1] Timers: close idle connections on time
labels: week:4, size:M
groups: B

**Size:** M (2-3 days) · **Needs:** W3.B3 · **Language topic:** `std::chrono`, a min-heap with `std::priority_queue`

## What is this, in plain words?
An event loop must close idle connections too, but it can't sleep or block
per connection. Instead it keeps a **list of deadlines**, and uses the nearest
one as `epoll_wait`'s timeout. When `epoll_wait` returns, it closes every
connection whose deadline has passed.

## Step by step
1. Add `--idle-timeout-ms N` (default 5000).
2. Each `Connection` remembers `last_active` (a `std::chrono::steady_clock::time_point`), updated whenever data arrives or a response is sent.
3. Simple version first: before each `epoll.wait`, find the earliest deadline (`last_active + timeout`) over all connections, and use the time until then as the timeout. After `wait`, close every connection that's past its deadline. That's O(n) per loop; fine for a start.
4. Better version: a `std::priority_queue` of `(deadline, fd)` (a min-heap). Entries for connections that became active again are just skipped when they reach the top ("lazy deletion").
5. Uncomment `b_idle_timeout` in `tests/CMakeLists.txt`.
